Most multifactor portfolios combine a small number of signals with fixed
weights. That is easy to understand, but it treats every signal as equally
useful through time. Ridge regression offers a simple alternative: estimate the
combination from the data while shrinking correlated coefficients toward zero.

The goal of this study is to find out whether that additional flexibility is
useful in practice. I compare Ridge with a fixed-weight multifactor score using
the same stock universe, execution timing, portfolio construction, transaction
costs, and rebalance dates. This keeps the comparison focused on the score used
to rank stocks.

## Research questions

The analysis is organized around four questions:

1. Does Ridge change the average or the stability of the out-of-sample stock
   ranking, measured by the daily information coefficient?
2. Does that difference carry through to net return, risk, and drawdown after
   portfolio construction and trading costs?
3. Which predictors receive the largest coefficients, and how stable are those
   coefficients across walk-forward fits?
4. Which predictor exposures remain in the final long-short portfolio?

I first define the two scores and the expanding walk-forward design. I then
compare information coefficients and portfolio performance before examining
the fitted coefficients and realized portfolio exposures.

## The two scores

The benchmark gives equal weight to five signed cross-sectional ranks:
defensive risk, momentum, short interest, size, and return consistency. The two
defensive measures are averaged before the five themes receive their 20%
weights.

Ridge uses 191 individual predictor columns: overlapping windows of returns,
volatility, price-to-moving-average ratios, RSI, market capitalization, volume,
short interest, and correlation with the Russell 1000. Each column enters
separately, so the model can choose among different horizons of the same input.

Both scores use the point-in-time Russell 1000 universe, select 75 longs and
75 shorts, apply the same volatility scaling, trade at the next close, pay 5 bp
per dollar traded, and run on three Friday rebalance calendars.

## Walk-forward validation

The target is the stock's risk-adjusted return over the next 20 sessions. It is
ranked within BICS sector on each date. Predictors are separately ranked across
the universe on the same date and mapped to $[-1,1]$.

The model begins with 900 market dates of training data, leaves a 21-date gap,
scores the following 600 dates, and then expands the training history. Each
training window contains three complementary every-third-date samples whose
predictions are averaged.

## Information coefficient

The information coefficient (IC) is the daily cross-sectional Spearman
correlation between a score and the subsequently realized target. It evaluates
the stock ordering before position sizing and trading.

The two scores have effectively the same mean daily IC. Ridge is more
consistent from one date to the next, which lowers IC volatility and raises the
IC information ratio. The score-level evidence is therefore about stability,
not a larger average rank correlation.
