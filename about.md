I'm Pieter-Jan Inghelbrecht. I write about systematic equity research, with a
particular interest in what happens between a stock forecast and an implemented
portfolio.

The questions I keep returning to are practical. Which signals add information
once their overlap is accounted for? How much risk does a position contribute
alongside the rest of the book? When is a replacement worth its trading cost?
And when a portfolio loses money, which exposures actually explain the loss?

The notes follow those questions through stock ranking, position sizing,
rebalancing, and constrained optimization. I include the assumptions and
unsuccessful comparisons because they determine what I would do with the
result. A higher backtest Sharpe is useful only if I can explain where it came
from and what could make it disappear.

For a starting point, the [low-volatility
study](/quant/2024/12/15/low-volatility-factor.html) shows how changing position
sizes changes the risk of the same selected stocks. The
[optimizer study](/quants/2026/08/29/portfolio-optimization.html) extends that
question to the whole portfolio and the cost of changing its holdings.

You can find my public research code on [GitHub](https://github.com/piinghel)
and the papers and software behind these notes on the
[resources page](/quants/2025/04/20/reading-and-tools.html).
